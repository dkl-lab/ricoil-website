The Law What Happens To Your Oil FAQs
Compliance

Why Compliant Disposal Isn't Optional

Waste cooking oil is classed as controlled waste under the Environmental Protection Act 1990. That puts a legal Duty of Care on every food business that produces it — one that doesn't end the moment a collection vehicle drives away.

Duty of Care

You stay responsible after collection

Under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, the business producing the waste remains liable if it's later handled illegally — even after handing it to a collector. Using an unregistered carrier doesn't transfer that risk away from you.

Registered carriers

Check who's collecting

Anyone collecting waste oil from your premises must be registered with the Environment Agency as a waste carrier. You can verify any carrier's registration on the EA's public register before you let them near your storage.

Paperwork

Waste Transfer Notes

Every legitimate collection should come with a Waste Transfer Note recording what was collected and where it's going. Keep these for at least two years — it's your proof the oil left your business responsibly.

Storage

Sealed, labelled, separate

Waste cooking oil should be stored in sealed, clearly labelled containers, kept apart from other waste streams and away from drains. Never pour it away — it solidifies in pipework and is a leading cause of sewer blockages and fatbergs.

This is a general guide, not legal advice. Requirements can vary by nation (England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each have their own environmental regulator) and by the volume of oil you handle. If you're unsure how the rules apply to your business, check the Environment Agency's guidance or speak to a compliance professional.

Common Questions

FAQs

Do I legally need a licensed collector, or can I dispose of oil myself?

You need a registered waste carrier. Because waste cooking oil is controlled waste, handing it to anyone unregistered — or disposing of it yourself incorrectly — leaves your business exposed to liability even if you didn't know the collector wasn't registered.

How often will my oil be collected?

Whatever suits your volume — from a fixed weekly or fortnightly round for busy kitchens to on-request collection for smaller or seasonal producers. We'll agree a schedule based on how much oil you actually go through.

What if my storage container is hard to get to?

That's what our pump-assisted extraction is for. If your container is boxed in, awkward to shift, or just inconvenient to reach, our team extracts the oil directly rather than treating it as a reason to skip your collection.

Is there a cost, or could I get paid for my oil?

It depends on your volume and how consistently you produce it — this is exactly the kind of detail we work out with you directly rather than quoting blind. Get in touch and we'll give you a straight answer for your kitchen.

What's the Oil Exchange Programme?

For kitchens that go through a set quantity of fresh oil regularly, we can supply that oil back to you at a competitive rate as part of your collection — turning your waste stream into a saving on stock you'd be buying anyway.

What documentation will I get?

A Waste Transfer Note for every collection, confirming what was collected and where it's going. Keep these on file — it's your legal proof of compliant disposal if it's ever checked.

Can I sign a long-term agreement rather than one-off collections?

Yes — most of our accounts run on multi-year agreements. It gets you a locked-in rate and a fixed schedule, and gets us the accountability to keep service consistent for the long run.
